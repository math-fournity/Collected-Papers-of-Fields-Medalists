# CERTAIN DIOPHANTINE EQUATIONS

By

I. Introduction. The most popular diophantine equation

is perhaps the equation

$$
c _ {F _ {n}}: z _ {1} ^ {n} - z _ {2} ^ {n} = z _ {3} ^ {n}
$$

where n is a natural number.

A (non trivial) solution of $C_{F_n}$ is a triple $(z_1, z_2, z_3) \in \mathbb{Z}^3$ with $z_1 \cdot z_2 \cdot z_3 \neq 0$ and $\gcd(z_1, z_2, z_3) = 1$ and satisfying $C_{F_n}$. It is well known that for $n = 1, 2$ there are infinitely many solutions of $C_{F_n}$, for $n = 3$ (and many other exponents) there are no solutions, and due to Falting's theorem and the fact that the genus of the curve defined by $C_{F_n}$ is larger than 1 for $n > 3$ it follows that there are only finitely many solutions for $n > 3$.

The famous conjecture denoted as "Fermat's last theorem" is

CONJECTURE  $F_{n}$ . For  $n \geq 3$  there are no solutions of  $C_{F_{n}}$ .

It is easily seen that the essential case is that $n = p$

is a prime.

If one tries to prove $\underline{\mathbf{F}}_{\mathfrak{p}}$ it soon becomes obvious that

the arithmetic of the cyclotomic field $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\mathrm{p}})$ where $\zeta_{\mathrm{p}}$ is

a primitive p-th root of unity plays an important role.

This observation first made by Kummer was a great stimu-

lus for the development of algebraic number theory and

especially of class field theory: One studies unramified

abelian extensions of  $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\mathfrak{p}})$  whose degree is a p-power,

and in the "regular case" where no such extensions exist,

it follows that  $F_{D}$  is true (cf. [1]). In other words:

The non-existence of 1-dimensional unramified represen-

tations of the absolute Galois group  $G_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})}$  of  $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})$  in

$G1_{1}(C)_{p}$  implies  $F_{p}$ .

So it seems to be natural to look for higher dimensional

representations $\rho$ of $G_{\mathbb{G}(\zeta_p)}$ with only small ramification,

and the first case to test is: $\rho$ has dimension 2 and is

induced by the action of  $G_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})}$  on the points of order p

of an elliptic curve E defined over Q.

There is our motivation to relate an elliptic curve E

with remarkable arithmetical properties to any solution

of equations of "Fermat type" (see II). Especially E is

stable over Q (cf. [2]). In the case of the original

Fermat equation the properties of E are so excellent

that one suspects that such a curve cannot exist (cf.

the Theorem on p. 15).

Now one has to attack those elliptic curves, and here

the conjecture of Taniyama and Shimura enters: Each

elliptic curve E over Q should be a quotient of the

Jacobian of an appropriate modular curve $X_{0}(N)$. The re-

sulting parametrization

$$
\varphi : \mathrm{X} _ {\mathrm{o}} (\mathrm{N}) \longrightarrow \mathrm{E}
$$

carries all essential arithmetical data of E: If we

choose N to be minimal with respect to E then N is the

geometric conductor of E.

If $\omega$ is a holomorphic differential on $E$ then $\varphi^{*}\omega$ corresponds-

ponds to a modular form f of weight 2 on X(N), and this

form f determines the representations of  $G_{0} := \text{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{G}}/\mathbb{Q})$

induced by the action of  $G_{n}$  on the torsion points of E,

and the third essential data for us turns out to be the

degree of φ.

In III. we describe those facts in more detail and we

state two types of conjectures that would imply Fermat's

last theorem. The first type is due to Serre and is in

the spirit of the representation-theoretical point of

view we described above; it emphasizes the special role

of the equation $C_F$ amongst all other equations of this

type. The second type of conjectures states that the de-

gree of $\varphi: X_{0}(N) \longrightarrow E$ should have a polynomial bound

in N; it is due to Szpiro and supported by the "function

'field case". If this conjecture would be true it would

have consequences for all equations of "Fermat type" at

least for large exponents.

Both types of conjectures have links to so called "con-

gruence primes" (cf. [6]), and we give results about

these primes in IV. using results of Deligne, Rapoport

and Mazur about the Néron model of  $J_{0}(N)$  over Z in the

case that N is square free.

II. Construction of stable elliptic curves

1. Arithmetic of elliptic curves. In the following we

list some notions and properties concerning elliptic

curves over Q that are relevant for us. For a systematic

study of elliptic curves over global fields we refer to

[9].

An elliptic curve E/C is a projective irreducible curve

of genus 1 with (at least) one Q-rational point F. The

theorem of Riemann-Roch implies that one can find plane

projective curves of degree 3 which are models for E

such that  $P_{\infty}$  is the only "infinite" point of E. The equa-

tions for these models can be chosen in normal forms

whose affine versions we write down:

The generalized Weierstraß normal form:

E is given by

$$
\mathrm{N} _ {\mathrm{E}}: \mathrm{Y} ^ {2} + \lambda \mathrm{XY} + \mu \mathrm{Y} = \mathrm{X} ^ {3} + \alpha \mathrm{X} ^ {2} + \beta \mathrm{X} + \gamma
$$

with $\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \lambda, \mu \in \mathbb{Z}$ such that $N_E$ has no singular points.

There is a biregular transformation defined over $\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{2}]$ of

the affine plane such that E is given by

$$
\mathrm{N} _ {\mathrm{E}} ^ {\prime}: \quad \mathrm{Y} ^ {2} = \mathrm{X} ^ {3} + \mathrm{AX} ^ {2} + \mathrm{BX} + \mathrm{C}
$$

where again A,B,C are elements of Z and the non singularity of E now means: The discriminant of the polynomial  $X^{3}+AX^{2}+BX+C$  is different from O.

If we allow transformations of type: X  $\longrightarrow$  X' +  $\frac{1}{3}$ A we

get the usual Weierstraß normal form of E:

$$
\mathrm{N} _ {\mathrm{E}} ^ {\prime \prime}: \mathrm{Y} ^ {2} = \mathrm{X} ^ {3} - \mathrm{g} _ {2} \mathrm{X} - \mathrm{g} _ {3}
$$

where  $g_{i} \in Z$  without loss of generality and

$$
\Delta := 4 g _ {2} ^ {3} - 2 7 g _ {3} ^ {2} \neq 0.
$$

The only transformations that preserve the Weierstraβ normal form are of the type: X → X' = μ²X, Y → Y' = μ³Y with μ ∈ Q\*. Then g₂ is transformed to μ⁻⁴g₂ and g₃ to μ⁻⁶g₃. Hence, the discriminant Δ of X³-g₂X-g₃ is transformed to μ⁻¹².Δ.

The Weierstraß normal form is very convenient for de-

fining the invariants of E (determining E completely

over Q): The absolute invariant $j_{E}$ of E is given by

$$
j _ {E} := 1 2 ^ {3} \cdot 4 g _ {2} ^ {3} \cdot \Delta^ {- 1},
$$

and the Hasse invariant $\delta_{E}$ of $E$ is given by

$$
\delta_ {E} := - \frac {1}{2} g _ {2} \cdot g _ {3} ^ {- 1} \mod Q ^ {* 2} \quad i f j _ {E} \neq 0, 1 2 ^ {3}.
$$

(For all elliptic curves that occur in this paper we have that $j_{E} \notin \mathbb{Z}$ and hence $j_{E} \neq 0, 12^{3}$.)

A most effective tool to study elliptic curves over $\mathbf{Q}$ is the reduction theory:

We give by an equation in generalized Weierstraß normal form:

$$
N _ {E}: Y ^ {2} + \lambda X Y + \mu Y = X ^ {3} + \alpha X ^ {2} + \beta Y + \gamma
$$

with coefficients in Z. Let 1 be a prime and $\overline{\alpha},\overline{\beta},\overline{\gamma},\overline{\lambda},\overline{\mu}$ the residue classes in $\mathbb{F}_1 = Z / 1$ of the corresponding coefficients. Then

$$
\mathrm{N} _ {\mathrm{E}} (1): \mathrm{Y} ^ {2} + \overline {{\lambda}} \mathrm{XY} + \overline {{\mu}} \mathrm{Y} = \mathrm{X} ^ {3} + \overline {{\alpha}} \mathrm{X} ^ {2} + \overline {{\beta}} \mathrm{X} + \overline {{\gamma}}
$$

defines a plane affine curve over $\mathbb{F}_1$ whose projective closure $E^{(1)}$ has three possibilities:

1. "Good reduction case": $E^{(1)}$ is an elliptic curve again, i.e. it has no singular points. In case that $\lambda, \mu$ and $\alpha$ are equal to zero and $1 \neq 2,3$ there is a simple criterion for this case: We have $v_1(j_E) \geq 0$ and $v_1(4\beta^3 - 27\gamma^2) = v_1(\Delta) = 0$.

DEFINITION. E has good reduction mod 1 if there is an equation $N_E$ for E such that $N_{E(1)}$ defines an elliptic curve over $\mathbb{F}_1$.

2. "Multiplicative type": In this case  $E^{(1)}$  has a singu-

larity in exactly one point and there are two differ-

ent tangent lines in that point. The typical case is:

$$
\mathrm{v} _ {1} (\lambda) = 0, \mathrm{v} _ {1} (\mu) > 0, \mathrm{v} _ {1} (\alpha) > 0, \mathrm{v} _ {1} (\beta) > 0 \text { and } \mathrm{v} _ {1} (\gamma)
$$

$$
= - v _ {1} \left(j _ {E}\right) > 0.
$$

DEFINITION. E has reduction of multiplicative type mod 1

if there is an equation  $N_{E}$  for E such that  $N_{E}(1)$  defines

a curve with one singular point and different tangent

lines in that point.

E has reduction of multiplicative type if and only if

$v_{1}(j_{E}) < 0$ and $Q(\sqrt{\delta}_{E})$ is unramified in 1.

3. "Additive type": This is the "worst" case. Again $N_{E}(1)$

defines a curve over $\mathbb{F}_1$ with one singular point but

now there is only one tangent line through this point.

The description of cases 1. and 2. shows that after a finite

constant field extension the reduction type of E modulo

an extension of l is of type 1. or 2., and thereafter

the type never changes again. For this reason one says:

E has stable reduction mod 1 if there exists a model of

E that has good reduction or reduction of multiplicative

type mod 1.

The notion "multiplicative type" (resp. "additive type")

derives from the fact that the non singular points of  $E^{(1)}$

are (as algebraic groups) isomorphic to a torus split by

an extension of degree $\leq 2$ (resp. to the additive group) over $\mathbb{F}_1$.

The most important fact about elliptic curves with reduction of multiplicative type is due to Tate: Let K be a finite extension field of the field  $Q_{1}$  of l-adic numbers with  $\delta_{E} \in K^{\times2}$  and assume that E has reduction of multiplicative type mod 1. Then the group of K-rational points of E, E(K), is analytically isomorphic to  $K/\langle q\rangle$  where q, the l-adic period of E, is an element in  $Q_{1}$  with  $j_{E} = \frac{1}{q} + \sum_{i>0} a_{i} q^{i}$ . The elements  $a_{i}$  are the integers occurring in the usual Fourier expansion of the (classical) j-function over C (with  $q = e^{2\pi i\tau}$ ). As reference for this theory we mention [7].

We only need the following consequence: Let $E / \mathbb{Q}$ be an elliptic curve with reduction of multiplicative type mod 1. Let $E_p$ denote the group of points of order dividing $p$ of $E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})$, and denote by $K_p$ the field obtained by adjoining the coordinates of points in $E_p$ to $G$. Let $l$ be a divisor of $l$ in $K_p$ and $K_{p,l}$ the completion of $K_p$ with respect to $l$. Then $K_{p,l} = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p, P \vee j_E)$.

For $1 \neq p$ and the case that $E$ has good reduction mod $l$. Hensel's lemma gives information about $K_{p,l}$, and especially we get: $l$ is unramified in $K_p$.

For brevity's sake we make the

DEFINITION. An elliptic curve E over G is stable if it

has stable reduction modulo all primes.

The considerations made above prove the following

PROPOSITION 1. Let E be a stable elliptic curve defined

over G. Let  $K_{p}$  be the field extension of Q obtained by

adjoining the coordinates of points in  $E_{p}$ . Then  $K_{p}$  is

unramified over $\mathbb{G}$ in all primes $1 \neq p$ with $v_{1}(j_{E}) \geq 0$

or  $v_{1}(j_{E}) \equiv 0 \mod p$ .

If E is stable we find for all primes 1 a generalized

Weierstraß equation with integral coefficients such that

the reduction mod 1 of this equation is optimal. Since Z

is a principal domain we find one generalized Weierstraβ

equation corresponding to E with optimal reduction be-

haviour modulo all primes simultaneously. This equation

is called a "minimal Weierstraβ equation", and from now

on we will assume that

$$
\mathrm{N} _ {\mathrm{E}}: \mathrm{Y} ^ {2} + \lambda \mathrm{XY} + \mu \mathrm{Y} = \mathrm{X} ^ {3} + \alpha \mathrm{X} ^ {2} + \beta \mathrm{X} + \gamma
$$

with $\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \lambda, \mu \in \mathbb{Z}$ is a minimal equation. The discrimi-

nant of  $N_{E}$  is called the discriminant of E and denoted

by  $\Delta_{E}$ .

The following facts hold:

Assume that E is stable. Then  $v_{1}(\Delta_{E}) = -\text{Min}(v_{1}(j_{E}), 0)$  for

all primes 1, and the geometric conductor of E is equal

$$
\text { to   } N := \prod_ {v _ {1} (\Delta_ {E}) > 0} 1.
$$

(For the definition of the conductor we refer to [9].)

2. Stable elliptic curves. Now we want to construct

elliptic curves E with stable reduction modulo all primes

such that the corresponding field  $K_{n}$  has only small rami-

$$
\mathbf {K} _ {\mathbf {p}}
$$

fication over $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\mathbf{p}})$.

We begin with relatively prime integers A and B such

that A ≡ 0 mod  $2^{5}$  and B ≡ 1 mod 4 and put C := A-B.

Let $E$ be the elliptic curve given by the equation

$$
\mathrm{N} _ {\mathrm{E}}: \mathrm{Y} ^ {2} = \mathrm{X} ^ {3} + (\mathrm{A} + \mathrm{B}) \mathrm{X} ^ {2} + \mathrm{ABX}.
$$

A Weierstraß normal form of E is given by the equation

$$
N _ {\widetilde {E}}: \widetilde {Y} ^ {2} = \widetilde {X} ^ {3} - \frac {1}{3} (A ^ {2} + B ^ {2} - A B) \widetilde {X} + \frac {1}{2 7} (A + B) (2 A ^ {2} + 2 B ^ {2} - 5 A B).
$$

The discriminant of $N_{\widetilde{E}}$ is equal to $\Delta = A^2 \cdot B^2 \cdot C^2$.

Hence the j-invariant of E is equal to

$$
j _ {E} = 2 ^ {8} \left(A ^ {2} + B ^ {2} - A B\right) ^ {3} \cdot A ^ {- 2} \cdot B ^ {- 2} \cdot C ^ {- 2}
$$

and the Hasse invariant of E is equal to

$$
\delta_ {E} \equiv \frac {1}{2} \cdot \frac {A ^ {2} + B ^ {2} - A B}{(A + B) (2 A ^ {2} + 2 B ^ {2} - 5 A B)} \mod G ^ {* 2}.
$$

From those data we can verify the following properties

of E:

i) The points of order 2 of E are Q-rational and are

equal to  $P_{0} = (0,0)$ ,  $P_{1} = (-A,0)$ ,  $P_{2} = (-B,0)$  where  $P_{i}$

are points in  $N_{E}^{\prime}$ .

$$
\mathrm{ii}) \mathrm{v} _ {2} (\mathrm{j} _ {\mathrm{E}}) = 8 - 2 \mathrm{v} _ {2} (\mathrm{A}) \leq - 2 \text { and } \delta_ {\mathrm{E}} \equiv \mathrm{Bmod} \mathbb {Q} _ {2} ^ {* 2}.
$$

By assumption we have: B ≡ 1 mod 4 so that E has reduc-

tion of multiplicative type mod 2.

Now assume that 3/A·B·C. Then A-B = C ≠ 0 mod 3 and so

A $\nmid$ B mod 3. Hence the polynomial $X^3 + (A + B)X^2 + ABX$ has 3

different zeros mod 3 and $N_{E(3)}$ is a curve without sin-

gularities and E has good reduction mod 3.

In the case that 3|A·B·C we get: $v_3(j_E) = -v_3(\Delta) < 0$ and

$v_{3}(\frac{1}{2}\frac{A^{2}+B^{2}-AB}{(A+B)(2A^{2}+2B^{2}-5AB)})=0\text{ and hence } Q(V\delta_{E})\text{ is unrami-}$

fied in the divisors of 3: E has reduction of multipli-

cative type mod 3.

If 1 is a prime different from 2 and 3 then (since

mod 1 if and only if $v_{1}(j_{E}) \geq 0$, and $v_{1}(j_{E}) < 0$ if and

only if  $1|A^{2}\cdot B^{2}\cdot C^{2}$ . In the latter case it follows that

$v_{1}(\delta_{E}) = 0$ and hence E has reduction of multiplicative

type mod 1.

It follows: E is a stable curve over Q and the conductor

of E is equal to

$$
N = \prod_ {1 \mid A \cdot B \cdot C} 1.
$$

iii) It is easy to find a minimal equation for E:

$$
\mathrm{N} _ {\mathrm{E}}: \mathrm{Y} ^ {2} + \mathrm{XY} = \mathrm{X} ^ {3} + \frac {\mathrm{A} + \mathrm{B} - 1}{4} \mathrm{X} ^ {2} + \frac {\mathrm{A} \cdot \mathrm{B}}{1 6} \mathrm{X}
$$

is such an equation, the discriminant of $E$ is equal to

$$
\Delta_ {E} = \frac {A ^ {2} \cdot B ^ {2} \cdot C ^ {2}}{2 ^ {8}}.
$$

iv) Let $p$ be any odd prime and let $K_p$ be the field ob-

is unramified outside of divisors of p.

We specialize the numbers A, B, C.

For relatively prime integers $a_1, a_2, a_3$ and $n_1, n_2, n_3 \in \mathbb{N}$.

we define a Fermat type equation: C:  $a_{1}Z_{1}^{n_{1}} - a_{2}Z_{2}^{n_{2}} = a_{3}Z_{3}^{n_{3}}$

We assume that $(z_{1}, z_{2}, z_{3})$ is a solution of $C$ with

$\gcd(z_1, z_2, z_3) = 1, 2^5 |a_1z_1^n_1$ and $a_2z_2^n_2 \equiv 1 \mod 4$.

Put $A = a_1 z_1^{n_1}$, $B = a_2 z_2^{n_2}$ and $C = a_3 z_3^{n_3}$. Then the corres-

ponding elliptic curve is stable with discriminant

\Delta_ {E} = 2 ^ {- 8} \cdot a _ {1} ^ {2} a _ {2} ^ {2} a _ {3} ^ {2} \cdot z _ {1} ^ {2 n _ {1}} \cdot z _ {2} ^ {2 n _ {2}} \cdot z _ {3} ^ {2 n _ {3}}

It follows:

\frac {2 ^ {8} \Delta_ {E}}{N} > a _ {1} a _ {2} a _ {3} \cdot z _ {1} ^ {2 n _ {1} - 1} \cdot z _ {2} ^ {2 n _ {2} - 1} \cdot z _ {3} ^ {2 n _ {3} - 1}.
$$

Assume that E/Q is a stable elliptic curve with Q-ratio-

nal points of order 2. Assume moreover that $K_{n}$ is rami-

\mathrm{N} _ {\mathrm{E}}: \mathrm{Y} ^ {2} = \mathrm{X} ^ {3} + \mathrm{AX} ^ {2} + \mathrm{BX}

be an equation for E with A, B ∈ Z and gcd(A, B) = 1. The

The discriminant of  $X^{3}+AX^{2}+BX$  is equal to  $B^{2}(A^{2}-4B)$  and

The greatest common divisor of B and  $A^{2}-4B$  is equal to 1

$$

and

$$

Now assume that, say, $\alpha = 0$. Then $2|z_1$ and it would fol-

low that 2|B and 2|A.

Hence $\alpha = \beta = 1$. As a consequence we get:

$(z_{1},z_{2},v)$ is a solution of Fermat's equation.

are equivalent:

1. There exists a solution of Fermat's equation:

$$
z _ {1} ^ {p} - z _ {2} ^ {p} = z _ {3} ^ {p}.
$$

2. There exists a stable elliptic curve E over Q such

that

i) the points of order two of E are Q-rational,

ii) the field  $K_{p}$  obtained by adjoining the coordinates of the points of order p to Q is unramified outside 2·p,

iii) $\operatorname{Min}(0, \mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{p}}(j_{\mathbf{E}})) \equiv 0 \equiv \mathbf{v}_2(j_{\mathbf{E}}) - 8 \mod p$.

3. There exists a stable elliptic curve E over Q with a minimal equation

$$
\mathrm{Y} ^ {2} + \mathrm{XY} = \mathrm{X} ^ {3} + \alpha \mathrm{X} ^ {2} + \beta \mathrm{X} \quad \text { with } \alpha , \beta \in \mathbb {Z}
$$

and

$$
2 ^ {8} \cdot \Delta_ {E} \in z ^ {2 p}.
$$

## III. Conjectures

1. Modular elliptic curves. For $N \in \mathbb{N}$ let $X_{0}(N)$ be the modular curve parametrizing elliptic curves with cyclic isogenies of degree $N$.

$X_{o}(N)$ is defined over Z, one gets a model over C in the following way: The group

$$
\Gamma_ {o} (N) := \left\{\left( \begin{array}{l l} a & b \\ c & d \end{array} \right) \in S 1 (2, Z); c \equiv 0 \mod N \right\}
$$

operates on

$$
\mathbb {H} ^ {*} := \{z \in \mathbb {C}; \operatorname{Im} (z) > 0 \} \cup \{i, \infty , q \in \mathbb {Q} \}
$$

in the usual way. Then  $X_{o}(N) \otimes C$  is equal to  $H^{*}/\Gamma_{o}(N)$  as

Riemann surface.

Modular forms of weight 2 and level N are holomorphic

functions on H with the transformation rule

$$
f \left(\frac {a z + b}{c z + d}\right) = (c z + d) ^ {2} f (z) \quad \text { for   all } \left( \begin{array}{l l} a & b \\ c & d \end{array} \right) \in \Gamma_ {o} (N)
$$

Then K := B ∩ A is a finite group scheme over Q with

$K \cap E_{p}^{*} = \{0\}$ by assumption. Define

$$
\psi : J _ {o} (N) \longrightarrow J _ {o} (N) / K = A / K \times B / K =: A ^ {\prime} \times B ^ {\prime}.
$$

$\psi$ maps the connected component of $\widetilde{J}_{o}(N)$ mod 1 to the connected component of $A' \times B'$ mod 1, and hence the components $C_{i}^{o}$ of the Néron model of $E^{*}$ mod 1 with $p \cdot C_{i}^{o} = C_{o}^{o}$ are mapped into the connected component of the unity of $A'$ mod 1 (cf. proof of prop. 4). Hence we have the following situation: There is an elliptic curve $E'/Q$ isogeneous to E with $v_{1}(j_{E'}) \equiv 0 \mod p$ which is a subvariety of an abelian variety $A'$ having totally degenerated reduction of multiplicative type mod 1, and $E_{p}'$ is contained in the connected component $A'^{o}$ of $A'$ mod 1, where 1 is a divisor of 1 in $K_{p}$.

There are an elliptic curve $\widetilde{\mathbf{E}}/\mathbf{Q}$ isogeneous to $\mathbf{E}$ with

$v_{1}(j_{\underset{E}{\sim}}) \equiv 0 \mod p$, an abelian variety B/Q and an isoge-.

ny $\alpha: \widetilde{E} \times B \longrightarrow A'$ defined over $Q$ with $\alpha(\widetilde{E}) = E'$ and

$$
\operatorname{Ker} (\alpha) \cap \widetilde {E} = \{0 \}
$$

Now we use the fact that $A'$ and $\widetilde{E} \times B$ have totally degenerated reduction mod 1: There is an unramified extension $K$ of $Q_1$ such that $A' \otimes K \cong G_m^d / \Gamma$ where $d = \dim(A')$ and $\Gamma$ is a lattice in $(K^*)^d$ uniquely determined by $A'$ and $(\widetilde{E} \times B) \otimes K \cong G_m / \langle q \rangle \times G_m^{d-1} / \Gamma_1$ with $\Gamma_1$ a lattice in $(K^*)^{d-1}$ and $q$ the l-adic period of $\widetilde{E}$; the iso-morphisms are in the category of rigid analytic spaces. Since $A'$ (resp. $\widetilde{E}$ and $B$) are defined over $Q_1$ we have for $\sigma \in G(K / Q_1) : \sigma \Gamma = \Gamma$ (resp. $\sigma \Gamma_1 = \Gamma_1$) and hence $G_m^d / \Gamma$.

$(\text{resp. } G_m / \langle q \rangle \times G_m^{d-1} / \Gamma_1)$ are abelian varieties defined over $G_1$. The isogeny $\alpha: \widetilde{E} \times B \longrightarrow A'$ induces an isogeny $\widetilde{\alpha}$ of $G_m / \langle q \rangle \times G_m^{d-1} / \Gamma_1$ to $G_m^d / \Gamma$ which is rational over $K$. But $\widetilde{\alpha}$ is induced by a mapping of the lattice $\langle q \rangle \times \Gamma_1$ to $\Gamma$ and hence is defined over $G_1$ already. To be more precise: For all finite overfields $L$ of $K$ we have analytic maps

$$
\begin{array}{l} \gamma_ {1}: (\widetilde {E} \times B) (L) \longrightarrow L ^ {*} / \langle q \rangle \times (L ^ {*}) ^ {d - 1} / \Gamma_ {1} \text { and } \\ \gamma_ {2}: A ^ {\prime} (L) \longrightarrow (L ^ {*}) ^ {d} / \Gamma \\ \text { with } \gamma_ {2} \circ \alpha = \widetilde {\alpha} \circ \gamma_ {1}. \end{array}
$$

Now take $P \in \widetilde{E}(K \cdot K_p)$ and $\sigma \in G(K_p \cdot K / G_1)$. Then we get

$$
\gamma_ {2} (\sigma (\alpha (P))) = \gamma_ {2} (\alpha (\sigma P)) = \widetilde {\alpha} (\gamma_ {1} (\sigma P)) = \epsilon (\sigma) \sigma (\widetilde {\alpha} (\gamma_ {1} (P)))
$$

with

$$
\varepsilon (\sigma) = \left\{ \begin{array}{l l} 1 & \text { if   } \widetilde {E} \text {   is   a   Tate   curve   over   the   fixed   field   of   } \sigma \\ - 1 & \text { else } \end{array} \right..
$$

$$
\text { Hence: } \gamma_ {2} (\sigma (\alpha (P))) = \varepsilon (\sigma) \sigma (\gamma_ {2} (\alpha (P))) \text { or: }
$$

For points $Q \in E_p'$ and $\sigma \in G(K_p \cdot K / G_1)$ we have

$$
\gamma_ {2} (\sigma Q) = \varepsilon (\sigma) \sigma (\gamma_ {2} (Q)).
$$

We know that $\gamma_{2}(Q)$ is contained in the connected component of the unity of $(K^{*})^{d}/\Gamma$, hence it can be represented by a tuple $(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{d}) \neq (1,\ldots,1)$ of p-th roots of unity.

Assume at first that $E'$ is a Tate curve over an extension $K_0$ of $Q_1$ (of degree $\leq 2$) such that $K_0$ contains no primitive $p$-th root of unity. Then there is an element $\sigma \in G(K_p \cdot K/K_0)$ such that for all $Q \in E_p'$ we get: $\sigma(Y_2(Q)) \nmid Y_2(Q)$. On the other side $\varepsilon(\sigma) = 1$ implies: $\sigma Q \nmid Q$. But since $v_1(j_{E'}) \equiv 0 \mod p$ there is a point $Q_0$ of order $p$ in $E_p'$ which is invariant under $\sigma$, and we get a contradiction. Next assume that $1 \equiv -1 \mod p$ and that $E'/Q_1$ is not a Tate curve. Take $\sigma$ as an extension of the generator of $G(G_1(\sqrt{\delta_E'})/C_1)$ where $\delta_E'$ is the Hasse invariant of $E'$. Then for all $Q \in E_p'$ we get:

$$
\gamma_ {2} (\sigma Q) = - \sigma (\gamma_ {2} (Q)) = \gamma_ {2} (Q)
$$

since $\sigma(\zeta_{i}) = \zeta_{i}^{-1}$ for all p-th roots of unity. Hence $\sigma Q = Q$ for all $Q \in E_{p}'$, and this is false for all points $Q$ which are not contained in the connected component of $E'$ modulo 1.
